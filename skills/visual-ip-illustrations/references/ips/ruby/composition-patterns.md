# Ruby Composition Patterns

Route id: `ruby`.
Route status: `active`.
Output path: `assets/<article-slug>-ruby/`.
Source authority: `source.md`.
Ruby identity note: preserve the energetic orange-bob-haired human IP with black cat-eye glasses, white or light inner top, red lips, confident friendly expression, hand-drawn black linework, and orange hand-drawn connection-line motif.
Wardrobe note: outfits may change, but the Ruby identity markers must remain stable; use the wardrobe library only when the user asks for clothing variants, avatars, stickers, or social assets.
Ruby route block: generic anime-girl drift, missing orange hair, missing black cat-eye glasses, wrong glasses shape, missing hand-drawn orange connection line, changed face identity, passive decoration, over-glam fashion poster, realistic photo drift, chibi drift, excessive text, route leakage, and copied composition all fail the route.
Save accepted Ruby output under `assets/<article-slug>-ruby/` with an ordered English slug filename such as `01-topic-name.png`.

## Composition Families

Use one family per image.

### Article Operator

Ruby is the human operator of one article idea. She connects, inspects, tunes, pulls, marks, bridges, or sorts the object that represents the core claim.

Best for: blog body illustrations, Notion docs, process notes, SparkPark practice notes.

### Connection Map

Ruby holds or pulls the orange connection line across 2-4 objects, regions, notes, posts, or decision points. The orange line is the main structure.

Best for: distribution, multilingual markets, workflow handoffs, product loops, audience paths, content systems.

### Wardrobe Variant

Ruby's outfit language changes while identity remains stable. Keep the same orange hair, black cat-eye glasses, light inner top, and orange line.

Best for: IP wardrobes, style exploration, social profile variants.

### Avatar

Square head-and-shoulders portrait. Face and glasses are large and unobstructed. Outfit cues appear at collar, shoulder, neck, scarf, jacket, strap, bass, or tote edge.

Best for: profile images, author cards, channel avatars.

### Transparent Sticker

Full-body or half-body Ruby on a removable chroma-key source, then locally converted to alpha. Keep the silhouette complete, centered, and easy to paste onto other layouts.

Best for: sticker packs, social images, article covers, reaction assets.

### Action Sticker Library

One action per sticker: wave, think, sprint, shine, play bass, hold sign, connect cable, point, celebrate, pause, debug, sort, launch, or react.

Best for: sticker packs and recurring social content.

### Mini Comic

2-3 simple frames where Ruby's action changes state. Use this only when the user asks for a comic or sequence; otherwise prefer one clear image.

## Supporting Object Pool

Use low-density objects that support Ruby's action:

- orange cable, plug, thread, map line, route arc
- notes, sticky cards, tags, labels, drafts
- lamps, keys, signs, stamps, markers
- small dashboards only as simplified paper-like props, not real UI
- tote bag, bass, boots, glasses, scarf, jacket, keyboard, book, mic, or small machine when relevant
- maps, shelves, gates, bridges, levers, scales, compasses, envelopes, buckets, lenses

## Spatial Rules

- Keep Ruby's face, glasses, hair, hands, and orange line unobstructed.
- Keep labels outside Ruby's body and away from the orange line whenever possible.
- Put the main orange line in a readable curve, not a dense tangle unless the concept is untangling.
- Use 1-3 supporting objects for article illustrations; avoid dense architecture diagrams.
- For avatars, crop at upper bust and keep shoulders/neckline visible.
- For stickers, leave padding on all sides and avoid ground shadows.

## Anti-Repeat Rules

- Do not reuse the global map composition unless the prompt is about markets, regions, or multilingual reach.
- Do not default to a fashion pose when the task is conceptual; make Ruby do the cognitive work.
- Do not reuse the same cable loop shape in every image; vary the arc, plug, route, or hand interaction.
- Do not copy the wardrobe contact sheet layout for article illustrations.
- Do not let supporting props carry the whole meaning while Ruby only smiles nearby.
